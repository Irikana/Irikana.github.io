Backwards: The Sliding Problem

Author: KeDao Xue
Created: May 22, 2026
Type: Handwritten Article
Tags: PhysicsThinking

I shared my experience in high school and called the method I used it in exams to get the correct equation from subjects "Backwards". Actually I have another sample to make a statement for Backwards, also found it in high school.

Consider a ball, which could be thought as a mass point slide down from somewhere height or otherwise, on angle, in a circle rail along with the side (Fig.I). After a while, it touched the bottom. Through the figure I, we could write down the energy conservation:

$$\dfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2 + mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$

It told us that we slide a mass $m$ from the height $h$, it has initial kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2$ while it has the initial velocity $v_0$, it also has an initial potential energy $mgh$ while the ground being thought as the surface with the potential $V=0$. At the moment it touched the bottom, its potential energy converted to its kinetic energy. By the way, the whole rail is absolutely smooth. Now we do one thing, skillfully in algebra, cross out the mass $m$ from both side of the energy conservation, times a number 2 on both side:

$$2gh = v^2 - v_0^2$$

This is very familiar, it is actually familiar to the result in a Uniform variable speed linear motion, if you will, the letter $g$ also means an acceleration, $h$ stands for a distance, $v$ and $v_0$ are velocities, we could barely write it down again as:

$$2ax = v^2 - v_0^2$$

And forget what we said, where we started from, the equation right here, it could be write down in the situation below, which is a kind of motion:

A mass $m$ with an initial velocity $v_0$, and we give it an acceleration $a$ to move faster, after a while it changed its position by a distance $x$.
