Backwards and Pendulum Symmetry

Author: KeDao Xue
Created: May 19, 2026
Type: Handwritten Article
Tags: None

I used to use a method to solve problems about proving equations or formulas, we call it "Backwards". We use the equation given as conditions to prove back to what we already know by logically calculating. As a sample, for a single pendulum we want to prove $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$, by using Backwards, we do this:

There is an set, so we square both sides of the equation, $(T)^2=(2\pi)^2\cdot\dfrac{l}{g}$ make it more simple, $gT^2=(2\pi)^2\cdot l$. Well, we could see an acceleration on the left side, that make us think about the Newton's second law $F=ma$, now we have an acceleration so we want to see what would happened if we create an $F$ into the equation. To do this, we times a mass on both sides of the equation, $mgT^2=m(2\pi)^2\cdot l$. Write down the second law more perfectly:

$$mg = m(2\pi)^2 \cdot l \cdot \dfrac{1}{T^2}$$

Wait, $\dfrac{(2\pi)^2}{T^2}$ is just the angular velocity $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ for the whole motion circle. Thus we have:

$$mg = m\omega^2 l$$

But it is NOT the correct $F=ma$ for the single pendulum, why is it? I actually found this problem in high school, then I had shown it to my mechanics teacher in university, professor pointed out the mistake out but I have completely forgot.

I was in high school, I tried to explain this situation, because I think, I believe there is a deep impressive relation between them. What I said was, the equation $mg=m\omega^2 l$ gives a kind of motion like one object doing around another object, created by gravity, see this Figure I.

Same, what we tried to prove, $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$ gives what we know by a little, see Figure II, a single pendulum. I am a person who's very like to say the word "Symmetry", so I would like to say, there is a symmetry between Fig I and Fig II to make them absolutely the same, completely the same in some way so that we could get $mg=m\omega^2 l$ from $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$.

By the day I graduated from my high school, I thought the symmetry is like, they are both doing motion around another object.

Figure notes: Fig I: Circular motion with gravity as centripetal force; Fig II: Simple pendulum motion
